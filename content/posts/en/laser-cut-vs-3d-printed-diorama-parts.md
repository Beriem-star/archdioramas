Laser cutters and 3D printers have changed how dioramas are made. Parts that once took an evening with a knife now come out of a machine in minutes. But each machine is good at different things, and choosing the wrong one wastes time and material.

Here is how we decide, part by part, on our builds.

## Quick comparison

| | Laser cutting | 3D printing |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Flat parts and engraved textures | Rounded, solid or complex shapes |
| Typical parts | Walls, floors, roofs, windows, fences, stairs | Furniture, figures, pots, ornaments, rocks |
| Speed | Very fast for sheet parts | Slower; hours for large parts |
| Typical materials | MDF, plywood, basswood, card, acrylic | PLA/PETG filament (FDM) or resin |
| File formats | SVG, DXF | STL, 3MF |
| Surface | Clean edges, slight burn marks on wood | Layer lines (FDM) or very smooth (resin) |

## When to use laser cutting

A laser cutter is a precise, very fast knife for flat sheets. It shines when parts are **flat and repeated**:

- **Walls and floors** with door and window openings cut perfectly square
- **Window frames, railings and fences** with thin, consistent bars
- **Stairs** made of stacked or slotted layers
- **Engraved textures**: bricks, stone blocks, wooden planks and tiles, engraved directly into the surface

Wood and MDF also take paint and weathering beautifully, and laser-cut slot-and-tab joints make assembly quick and square.

**Watch out for:** burn marks on wood edges (sand them or paint over them), very thin parts that can char or break, and material thickness, which must match your scale. Read our [scale guide](/blog/diorama-scales-explained/) to see what 2 mm or 3 mm sheets represent at different scales.

## When to use 3D printing

A 3D printer builds objects layer by layer, so it handles shapes a laser cannot:

- **Furniture**: sofas, chairs, tables and beds
- **Figures and animals**
- **Pots, bottles, lamps and ornaments**
- **Rocks, rubble and organic shapes**
- **Many identical small parts**, such as roof tiles or bricks, printed in batches

### FDM or resin?

- **FDM (filament) printers** are affordable and safe for home use. They are ideal for larger parts and furniture at 1:24 to 1:50. Layer lines can be reduced with a small layer height, sanding and primer.
- **Resin printers** capture extremely fine detail, ideal for figures and tiny ornaments. Resin must be handled with gloves in a well-ventilated space, then washed and cured.

## The best approach: combine both

On most of our dioramas, the **structure is laser-cut** and the **details are 3D-printed**. A typical split looks like this:

| Part of the scene | Method |
|---|---|
| Base frame and walls | Laser-cut MDF |
| Brick or stone texture | Laser-engraved, or a printed texture sheet |
| Windows and doors | Laser-cut wood or acrylic |
| Furniture and interior items | 3D-printed |
| Plants, pots, lamps | 3D-printed, plus natural materials |
| Figures | 3D-printed resin, or bought in scale |

This keeps the build fast (sheets cut in minutes) while the printed details add the realism.

## File formats explained

- **SVG** – a vector format most laser software reads (LightBurn, xTool, Glowforge and others). It can also be printed on paper as a cutting template.
- **DXF** – a CAD vector format, ideal if you edit plans in AutoCAD or similar programs.
- **STL** – the most common 3D-print format: a mesh of triangles you open in a slicer (Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer, Cura and others).
- **3MF** – a newer 3D format that can also store colours and print settings.
- **PDF** – printable templates for cutting by hand with a knife.

## Safety basics

- **Never laser-cut PVC, vinyl or other chlorine-containing plastics.** They release toxic, corrosive gas that also damages the machine.
- Never leave a laser cutter running unattended, and keep a fire extinguisher nearby.
- Use extraction or ventilation for both lasers and resin printers.
- Wear nitrile gloves when handling uncured resin.

## Summary

Use the laser for anything flat, square or engraved. Use the printer for anything round, solid or tiny. Combine them and you get the speed of one with the detail of the other.
